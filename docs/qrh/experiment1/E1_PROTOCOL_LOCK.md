## F and admission rules

F includes every applicable historical numerical table/result in the starting
source, verified or unverified, with its original mathematical contract and
label. FROZEN_PROGRAM_BOUNDARY.json records all fixed instance/spec/source
candidates; BASELINE_SOURCE_MANIFEST.json covers every tracked file.
An inventory row is not itself evidence that a statement was established.
Known-refuted statements and generated challenge placeholders cannot be
treated as true hypotheses. A failed official verification policy alone does
not refute a genuine legacy mathematical/literature fact.

Allowed in N/N⁺: all actual frozen results, their general quantified
mathematical consequences, new analytic arguments, exact symbolic allowance
functions, and ordinary scalar/calculus proofs from frozen bounds. A simple
proof of an elementary exp/log/rational inequality is not banned by tactic
name. A new general analytic theorem valid for a range of parameters may be
instantiated lawfully; it is not restricted to old published rows.

Forbidden in N/N⁺: a new program execution/input, a newly evaluated finite
quadrature/profile/search/table, or a new execution certificate, even when
source code is unchanged or its evaluation is disguised as many norm_num,
decide or native_decide lemmas. New Arb values are scouting only. New
verified numerical computation belongs in E, not N⁺.

Borderline rule, fixed before phase conclusions: a genuine analytic proof
from F that uniformly bounds a calibration function is allowed, even if
instantiation defeats E. Merely checking its new calibration instance by a
finite evaluator is a new provider. Ambiguous fixed-instance scalar proofs
will be shown in **both strict and permissive sensitivity columns**, with
their actual data dependence; they will not be reclassified after a win.
Uncomputed analytic integrals are legitimate bound functions if proved,
but do not automatically prove a numerical threshold or prime-existence
claim. Such implications/comparisons must themselves be established.
