## Crossovers and fixed margins

For a power row \(C e^{-gL}/L^r\) versus a polynomial row \(A/L^k\), the ratio is \((C/A)L^{k-r}e^{-gL}\); the log derivative has sign \(k-r-gL\). Restrict to the domain intersection. Each side of \(L=(k-r)/g\) is monotone, so if there is a root of ratio \(=1\) on that portion it gives its exact crossover; otherwise use any point or an endpoint on the portion to determine the order there. This applies to all listed and inherited log-power rows on their domains, not merely a grid. Ties at equality are allowed. D02 vs B0 power rows with the same logarithmic normalization have ratio \((C_2/C_0)e^{-L/32}\) and only possible crossover \(32\log(C_2/C_0)\), with strict advantage to D02 on its right. For the two early common rows \((13,.262)\) and \((13,.015)\) this lies between 91 and 92; other signed branches may beat either row in the transition. Two rows of the same exponent are compared by coefficient on their overlap. Asymptotic power improvements are not treated as grounds to delete stronger early log-power or finite bounds.

At a fixed higher-order Ramanujan margin compare onsets both against inheritance and against the matched B0-cap proof, which includes new B0 density improvements. For a fixed onset the positive \(P_d\) coefficients give strict comparisons as \(d\) increases. The matched B0 tests at the three new small-degree D02 starting points give strictly negative polynomial-minus-loss results in the certificate (ablation transcript); this locates the actual assumption effect, not an impossibility theorem. In the Mertens comparison the gains at \(L=200\) are more than 29 times versus the matched B0 power row, with increasing relative gain on their overlap. The paired calculation is not an independent blind baseline experiment. No claim is made that B0 logically cannot imply D02.
