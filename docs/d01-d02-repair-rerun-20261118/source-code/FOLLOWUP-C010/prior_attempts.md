## Substantive attempts and repairs

1. Direct substitution of the hypothetical majorant into prime
   partial summation is ineffective through its early threshold:
   the original finite-height CH2 floor beats it there, and the old
   QRH proof cannot be discarded when evaluating it. Transfer to an
   all-height cutoff by Mellin continuation fixed this. The density-
   free Perron bound was then replaced by all-height weighted tail
   densities. A coarse exponential bound on the density integral
   was superseded by integrated fourth-order Taylor majorants and
   decreasing upper-tail coefficients. A continuous version removes
   the last-bin limitation at infinity.
2. A naïve product/sum transfer integrated |psi-x|, costing roughly
   six to eight copies of the endpoint error in addition to the
   endpoint. Trying to integrate a real-variable truncated formula
   with a common T* is not justified by FKS Theorem 3.2. A fixed
   height plus strip allowance does work but produces a needless
   intermediate error. The two-pole Perron kernel yields instead
   the absolutely convergent identity S7. The same integrated error
   improves one-sided pi transfer and helps connect the early signed
   analytic interval. Retaining the deterministic prime-power drift
   rather than putting a square-root error of the same sign into
   both endpoints improves Ramanujan comparisons.
3. The early Ramanujan bottleneck is finite-height prime-counting
   precision and source real/integer conventions, not the new
   high cutoff. The exact endpoint computation found a negative
   real right-neighborhood of the integer candidate, contradicting
   the literal real interpretation of the older printed lower ray.
   Quotient sieving and an interval root bracket resolve it with
   the inherited finite integer assertion. That huge inherited
   range was not re-sieved. The signed finite li input was used
   only on its original range; its upper-only contribution overlaps
   the two-sided analytic proof.
4. For higher Ramanujan orders, fixed leading-margin comparisons
   conceal the B0 plateau near log 189. A factorial polynomial
   of degree 20, a certified finite anchor, separate scaled residual
   orders, and an analytic monotone tail resolve that comparison.
   A proposed refined coefficient 81 from log 49 failed locally
   in the constructed profile near log 168.718; 80.7 survived.
   This is a witness against that certificate, not against the
   actual inequality. Some attempted tighter high-order onsets
   also fell below the proposed margin at the left endpoint and
   were relaxed (e.g. B0 at 253.6 for coefficient 1412).
5. The early Mertens proof initially used only geometric power
   sum tails and a coarser real-cell grid. Improved signed theta
   tail integrals and a finer grid considerably improved it, and
   local fixed-prime interval computations recovered the precise
   earlier Dusart-coefficient thresholds. The coarser grid failed
   to reproduce .2 near 3.45 million for the products/1/p;
   exploratory prime-endpoint analysis indicated a cell-width
   overestimate, not a counterexample to Dusart. The inherited
   original .2 bounds were kept rather than silently weakened.
6. FKS Table 8 is used as its explicit published Corollary 2.9
   with a1=.77, not the .618 preprint branch. The apparently

   the principal high-order tables are at the stated literature
   trust tier. Neither the paper's computational density constants

## Remaining limitations / restart

joins, upward constants, and replay receipts are complete. No
global optimality of the quantitative profiles or of a B0 derivation
is claimed. The exact-onset statements are exceptions: their
minimality follows from immediate local failures below the exact
roots and a proved tail thereafter.

A further material analytic improvement would need sharper
weighted high-zero bounds or phase cancellation in the endpoint
spectral sum, not just tighter quadrature in the transfer. A proof
of new power savings beyond the two zero cutoffs is not furnished.
The early analytic Ramanujan margin could be improved by a new
signed prime count above the inherited li range or a better
finite-height bound; the direct hypothetical psi applies too far
out, and the transferred cutoff does not control the main
finite-height floor there. The late logarithmic versus power
Mertens constants remain a coefficient-onset tradeoff, not
standardized optimal numbers. If continuing, begin with the
continuous density and signed kernel formulas (S5, S7, M2),
then quantify any new spectral information on a checked compact
range and rejoin its analytic tail. Independently reviewing the
FKS parameter discrepancy and inherited large finite Ramanujan
integer range would improve the trust assessment. No independent
review is represented as completed by this submission.
