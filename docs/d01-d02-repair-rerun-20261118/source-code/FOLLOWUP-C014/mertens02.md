\[
  G_M(x)\le .01370x^{-1/8}\ (B_0),\qquad

## Entire-domain proof of power and inverse-log tails

For a positive-term ψ density bound \(V\) write \(B(L)=e^{cL}V(L)\). In the notation of analytic02.md put
\[
 C_w=B+e^{cL}(Z_1+1.84e^{-L}+b_+),\quad
 C_d=B+e^{cL}\big((1+1/L)(Z_k+1.84e^{-L})+b_+\big).
\]
Then (6) of mertens.md gives \(G_M\le e^{-cL}\max(C_w,C_d/(1-C_d e^{-cL}/L))\) by \(e^s-1\le s/(1-s)\) when \(s<1\). Every scaled monomial term decreases if \(m\le(d-c)L\) for \(u^m e^{-du}\); factors \(L/(L-M)\) decrease. The code guards this branch by branch; the set of eligible branches can only grow. Thus one rational check at each onset suffices, and the increasing candidate domains do not interrupt the inequality. For comparison, directly inserting D02 into (6) on \(L\ge200\) before the integrated improvement gives the main unsigned terms \(e^{-cL}[L^2+L^2/c+2L/c^2+2/c^3]/64\) for \(J_w\), and \(e^{-cL}[L+(L+1)/c+1/c^2]/64\) for \(J_1,\Delta\), plus their respective prime-power corrections. These come from the exact integrals of \(u^2 e^{-cu}\) and \((u+1)e^{-cu}\). Its endpoint alone has scaled coefficient \(L^2/64\ge625\) there, whereas the cap-based density coefficient, even with the integral correction, is below .226 throughout that ray. The limiting discrete coefficient is the final slab constant plus \(5.8\cdot10^{-12}\), not an optimal constant claim.

## Attempts and limitations

Compact \(L^2\) convergence and integration by parts remove the old strip and Perron integral losses. The first inverse-square bound .074 was reduced to .0503 using the rounded zero-count integral. The old exact 500-million limit did not reach \(e^{21.45}\), so the new sieve repairs the finite gap for the smaller bridge explicitly. Further improvements to the finite rays would need a still larger exact cutoff or better endpoint estimates past 2.1 billion. For the exponential tails the main constraint is the high-zero density estimate near the cap, not the integration or exponentiation. Inserting the direct D02 majorant into (6) is weaker (it incurs two numerator log powers before integration); the Mellin and density step changes the power in both families' endpoint bounds, while the integrated formula ensures no corresponding loss in the tail constant. A zero on the cap is not excluded by Mellin convergence; the transform can grow like the inverse cube of distance to the boundary with the stated input. No optimality or impossibility of other proofs is asserted. See comparison02.md for whole-domain comparisons.
