## Why the computation certifies whole real intervals

The odd segmented sieve marks the odd multiples starting at the larger
of $p^2$ and the first multiple in each segment, using base primes
generated by the elementary sieve up to (sqrt N). Every composite
has such a least prime factor. Every emitted prime is visited once.
No table external to the source is read.

The fixed-point type uses quantization by $2^{-56}$; products have
outward integer floors/ceilings of the extreme endpoint products,
and all multiplication overflow is checked. The relevant integer
loops, including sieve multiplication, have bounds far below
their respective word limits. A logarithm of a rational is computed
after binary normalization to $[1,2]$ using $2\operatorname{atanh}(z)$,
$z=(u-1)/(u+1)in[0,1/3]$, 30 terms, and positive discarded tail
$\le 2(1/3)^{61}/(1-1/9)<2^{-56}$. The same atanh formula with
$z=1/(2p-1)$ supplies $\log(p/(p-1))$ at small primes.

The harmonic enclosure of $\gamma$ uses
$H_m-\log m-1/(2m)\le\gamma\le H_m-\log m-1/(2m)+1/m^2$, $m=10^6$.
On each interval $[k,k+1]$ the trapezoid surplus for $1/t$ lies in
$[0,1/(6k^3)]$: integrate twice by parts with kernel
$(t-k)(k+1-t)/2$, using $0<f''\le2/k^3$. Sum and let the end tend
to infinity.

Write $C=\sum_{p\le Y}(\log(p/(p-1))-1/p)$, with (Y) the cut
implemented in the code. For $p>4096$ terms of degree 2,3,4 in the
positive log series are individually enclosed by rounded-down
integer divisions plus one unit each; the stated denominator cuts
omit only zero floors. The tail over all omitted terms past degree 4
is at most $1/[20(1-1/4096)4096^4]<20\cdot 2^{-56}$.
The tail past (Y) is at most
$\frac12\sum_{n>Y}1/[n(n-1)]=1/(2Y)$. Thus $M=\gamma-C-\mathrm{tail}$,
and the finite (log Q) is $S_0$ plus precisely this correction up to $x$
(with the appropriate positive tail if $x>Y$).
The linearization
$\log p=\log a+(p-a)/a+E, -((b-a)/a)^2/2\le E\le0$
allows summing ((log p)/p) in a block ([a,b]) using only its count
and reciprocal sum. The same stored log endpoints enclose
$\sum_{p\le Y}\log p/[p(p-1)]$; this sum plus $\gamma$ and its remaining
tail equals $B_3$. The remaining tail is in $[.99/Y,1.01/Y]$ for
$Y\ge10^7$: Dusart Thm. 4.2, $k=2,eta_2=.2$ starts at 3594641,
and for $Y\ge10^7$ its relative error is $<.001$.
Partial summation against $t^{-2}$ gives
$(1-.003)/Y\le\sum_{p>Y}\log p/p^2\le(1+.003)/Y$;
multiply the upper by $Y/(Y-1)$. In small test runs one may instead
use the elementary integral upper bound $2(\log Y+1)/Y$.

For every block, both the step function (any of the three sums) and
the reference ((loglog x) or (log x)) are increasing, so the
difference is between the lower-sum minus upper-reference and the
upper-sum minus lower-reference. Evaluate these bounds using outward
intervals and multiply the absolute bound by the largest log power
of the block. For (P,Q) apply $e^u-1\le u/(1-u)$ to the upper bound
of the absolute log-error before multiplying; this covers both
reciprocal products. The transcript prints integer upper roundings,
not estimates of the maximizers or extrapolations between sample
points. All endpoints in the closed bands are covered.
