# Mertens products and prime sums

Use real x with right-continuous prime cutoff, t=log x. Define P*=exp(gamma)*t*P_raw, Q*=1/P*, q=log Q*, u=sum_{p<=x}1/p−log t−M and j=sum_{p<=x}log(p)/p−t+B3. The constants are exactly Dusart2018 Thms 5.6–5.9 (PDF pp.17–20):
[
 M=\gamma+\sum_p[\log(1-1/p)+1/p],\qquad
 B3=\gamma+\sum_p\log(p)/(p(p-1)).
]
A power row (onset,c,b) bounds |P*−1|, |Q*−1| and |u| by c*x^(b−1)/t and |j| by c*x^(b−1), on the whole real ray. A log row (onset,c) instead uses c/t^3 and c/t^2. All table rows are strict at inclusive onsets, except the exact non-strict roots explicitly stated below. For the strongest proved envelope, take pointwise minima and intersect signed intervals, including all inherited results where applicable, not just the new table.

### Verification of sharp endpoints

The executable finite check in mertens_d02.py uses trial division up to sqrt(n) on n<10010. This is complete by factor pairing. The sums are exact intervals; on each prime-free gap test closed subcells with the prime sums fixed at the left prime. The right endpoint tested with that fixed sum gives a bound up to the left limit, and the next cell tests the right-continuous after-jump value. Restrict to [L,10000] including the precise intersection boundaries, with further bisection as needed. Thus no assumption about the shape of the absolute error between sample points is needed. Use the inherited D01-reviewed M and B3 brackets (.261497212838,.261497212858) and (1.332582275723,1.332582275744) from D02_mertens_sieve.py, independently established without QRH or a new exponent; the finite computation here does not elevate a numerical approximation to an input. For gamma use H_N−log N−1/(2N) as a lower bound, with upper error 1/(8N²), N=2^19. Indeed the trapezoid error on [n,n+1] for 1/x is positive and at most (f'(n+1)−f'(n))/8 by the integral kernel v(1−v)/2. Sum to infinity and use the definition of gamma.

The closed suffixes x>=119 and x>=285 are covered by that finite check and the analytic proof below. For the three unweighted roots put A=exp(−gamma)*Q_raw(113), V0=sum_{p<=113}1/p−M and k=5/32. There is no prime in (113,119], by trial division. On [117,119] the three positive scaled absolute errors are, in order Q,P,u,
[
 e^{kt}(A-t),\quad e^{kt}t(1-t/A),\quad e^{kt}t(V0-\log t).
]
They are strictly decreasing: differentiate in t; the scalar tests give A>log 119, V0>log log 119, k(A−log117)<1, (k log119+2)(1−log117/A)<1 and (k log119+1)(V0−log log117)<1. For the weighted case put B= B3+sum_{p<=283}log(p)/p. The entire [283,285] is free of further prime jumps, the positive scaled error is e^{kt}(B−t), and the scalar tests give B>log285 and k(B−log283)<1. Each shown bracket has rigorous opposite strict signs about .25665. Monotonicity proves both the root-to-checked-suffix join and failure just to the left. This sharpness is for the specified functional power tolerance, not for all possible errors or caps. For the original O*(.2/t³) and O*(.3/t²) tolerances the sharp D01 roots and signed bounds remain unchanged (mertens-new.md); no analytic input moves these exact roots.

## Proof of the analytic joins and constant caps

The exact Mertens Abel formulas and U,V,W definitions are in mertens-new.md. In brief, with D(t)=psi(exp t)/exp t−1 and S_a^* from theory_d02.md, set H(t)=D(t)−S_0^*(x) and K(t)=D(t)/t−integral_0^infty (1+a)S_a^*(x) da. Then j=H+W, u=K+V, q=K+U. Endpoint atoms are included; 0<=U<=V<=W/t. For x>=1e8 use W<=1.092/sqrt x, proved in D01_mertens.md. Any uniformly-in-a tail bound of the form |S_a^*|<=e^(-at)J(t) supplies J for H and (1/t+1/t²)J for K. For normalized power caps the extra contribution is (1+1/t)e^((1−b)t) J(t)+1.092 exp(−(b−.5)t). The code uses the valid refined 1/H inverse-mass option of theory_d02.md in norm(), or the simple J_b at earlier t.

For the power joins use the reviewed finite sqrt-scale cap 1.88 on [10000,1e8] (clipped first cell), and 1.94 on [1e8,1e13] by CH2/D01; descending normalized exponentials give their worst endpoints. In the B0 test start the finite portion at 560000. Put k=1−b. From log(1e13) through y use CH2 Cor.1.3 and freeze the factor 1+1/t in the tail at the start of that segment. After multiplying by exp(kt), the result is a sum of positive exponentials plus constants; hence convex. For D02 y=166.41, z=169.4; B0 y=185.14, z=186.8. Over [y,z] use the analytic h(t)=1.05e−12*exp(−k(t−y)/2) in the smoothing bound, freeze exp(h(t)) and 1+1/t at y and use a fixed Table 8/Dusart step choice for the refined tail. This gives another convex majorant in the normalized scale. Its endpoint values are checked; a pointwise optimizer is not assumed constant. On [z,infinity) use the positive exponential C_b normalized bound and the refined tail/spill expression. Every normalized component is nonincreasing on this ray. The last coefficient rows use the inherited continuous bound d_1(b)*t/(t−140) plus the low and truncation terms of power.md and the same refined tail; these normalized bounds likewise decrease. Convert a normalized log bound M0 for each segment starting at t0 to a normalized relative bound M0/(1−M0 exp(−kt0)/t0), using exp(v)−1<=v/(1−v), with strict checked positivity of the denominator. All segment endpoints are included.

For the log joins multiply the same unnormalized weighted radius and the log-product radius times t by t². Between 61 and y use CH2 point bound and J_b, without discarding the curvature: the result is a positive sum of t², and t or t² times exp(−rt) with r>=k. Each is convex there (r t>2+sqrt 2 suffices for t²). The maximum of this upper function is at an endpoint. From y onward use the decreasing t² smoothing upper bound of theory_d02.md and the spectral extra terms, also decreasing after this normalization. It is valid to continue this logarithmic argument on the whole ray including after z. For the much smaller later caps use the decreasing C_b normalized bound from the respective t0, times decreasing t² exp(−kt). For products apply the same exponential inequality with maximum v=cap/t0³. This explains why neither the log nor the power table is a set of numerical samples.

## Continuous pointwise improvement and side information

At a fixed x take the minimum of: all original signed/absolute point bounds in mertens-new.md's comparator (CH2 Cor.1.2–1.3/Lemma 9.2; finiteRH Cor.1; Dusart 4.2–4.5 and 5.6–5.9; PT2021 Theorem 1/Cor.1; FKS Theorems 1.1–1.2, Tables 5–6 and Cor.1.4), the complete inherited D01 branches, and the new signed transfers and table rays. Minimize first in lawful domains; even in the common formulas CH2 may still be selected where a spectral point bound is large. In D02 use theory_d02.md with b=27/32, and intersect with all B0 proof bounds; use signed Abel tails of pointwise Chebyshev intervals as alternatives to oscillatory tails as in mertens-new.md. For q in [l,r], keep [e^l−1,e^r−1] and [e^(−r)−1,e^(−l)−1] for Q*−1 and P*−1 before the final absolute envelopes. Exact signed values in the finite segments are available by the same quasiconstant-in-gap arithmetic, not only by the root tests. The resulting atlas is finite plus analytic; no optimality over all possible derivations is claimed. The two reciprocal products are correlated conclusions.

Every matched spectral kernel here narrows pointwise as b narrows (nested mesh and positive increments); integrated Abel kernels preserve its ordering. The original valid branches are not revoked. The exact raw point radius from D02 also may be inserted for t>=200, but is strictly worse than the new spectral radius by a factor >2800 throughout its own ray: check 2800*C_b(200)<625 and use monotonicity of normalized C_b and t². Its role is to prove the global strip, not to provide the smallest direct numerical radius. The strongest justified power rate is the one displayed for the strip mechanism, not a proof of best possible power saving; the strip leaves boundary zeros possible.

Two refinements for the signed branches:
1. In prime-power spill centering (mertens-new.md), at y=x^(1/m)>=exp(80) one may compare theta(v)−v for all v>=y against C_b(log y)*v^b+1.4262 sqrt(v). The C monotonicity proves this entire tail. Use the minimum in the positive partial-summation kernel with the inherited CH2 bound, including at its boundary. As v increases the new bound has strictly smaller main growth than the old CH2 line; this tightens remainder intervals about the same integral centers, without inferring a sign in the far tail. For x below the new legal domain keep the older centers or finite intervals.
2. There is a joint cancellation not captured by independent errors for j and u. For t>=80 put L_b=.00994 e^(-t/2)+K_b(t)/H+1.84 e^(-t), with K_b the **inverse-square radius** from theory_d02.md, not the Abel error K(t). Then
[
 |j-tu-(W-tV)|,\quad |j-tq-(W-tU)|
 \ \le J_b^{\rm ref}(t)/t+(1/t+2/t^2)L_b(t).
]
Indeed the difference e^(at)S_a^*−S_0^* has magnitude <=a L_b: for a zero use |1+a−rho|>=|1−rho| and the difference of reciprocal denominators; this costs a/|1−rho| beyond the inverse-square weight. For the low zeros on the line, |1−rho|^{-1}<=sqrt(.0462), by their positive total weight; .0462*sqrt(.0462)<.00994. For high ordinates the extra factor is <=1/H. For the constant and trivial terms use 1−exp(−av)<=av in the tail with s=t+v and integral of v*exp(−v). Finally H−tK= S_0^*/t+t integral_0^infty (1+a)e^(−at)(e^(at)S_a^*−S_0^*)da. This proves the claimed coupled bounds, allowing further intersections if one of the signed errors is bounded separately. Both high-ordinate weights can use the stronger strip; one extra logarithmic factor occurs in this particular difference, not in each separate leading error.

### Concrete limits

The smoothing proof loses power asymptotically because the support-error term h/2 trades against inverse-square mass divided by h. Earlier switch trials (e.g. using a convex segment ending at 169.65 with target .2569) failed; the verified join at 169.4 uses both sides and then switches to the unsmoothed proof. A coarse primitive cell bound stalled above 283 for the weighted .25665 row; adaptive clipping resolved the right suffix, while the exact value at 283 itself exceeds .2707 in normalized units. Claims extending this fixed weighted tolerance over 283 would be false. Bounding the integrated tail more tightly cannot generally pay for the point error D(t)/t, and the sign-sensitive difference result is not an improvement to that individual term. The support and density methods tried here do not remove another power of log t from the separate endpoint errors or exclude zeros on the boundary.
