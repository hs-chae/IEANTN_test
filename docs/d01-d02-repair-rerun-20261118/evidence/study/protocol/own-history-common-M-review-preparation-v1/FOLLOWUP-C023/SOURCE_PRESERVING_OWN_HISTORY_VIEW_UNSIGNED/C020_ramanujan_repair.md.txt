## Analytic pi enclosure

Set `F(u)=uπ(exp u)/exp u` (this is different from the D01 coefficient function called F there), and `H(u)=∑_{i=0}^{20}i!/u^i`. Use `li` principal value from zero. On u≥24,
`H(u)≤u li(exp u)/exp u≤H(u)+V(u)`.
The exact Taylor calculation in ram_repair.py brackets the unnormalized residual at 24 between 0 and 4. It uses `li(exp v)=γ+log v+∑_{n≥1}v^n/(n n!)` for v>0, with 0<γ<1. This identity follows from principal-value integration of exp(v)/v; equivalently the constant is γ from integrating (exp(t)−1)/t near zero and the elementary exponential-integral constant. The proof also works without identifying the constant as γ: `lim_{v↓0}(li(e^v)−log v)=∫_{−∞}^{−1}e^t/t dt+∫_{−1}^0(e^t−1)/t dt`, between 0 and 1 since the positive integral is at least 1/2 (e^t≤1+t+t²/2 for −1≤t≤0) and the negative magnitude less than e^−1, while the positive integral is at most 1. Thus the same rational residual test suffices. Differentiate exp(u)H(u)/u; its under-derivative against li is 21! exp(u)/u^22. Integrating by comparison with the derivative of exp(u)/u^22 from 24 gives V=4u e^−u+12·21!/u^21. For u≥40 also use the smaller available `1000u e^−u+(20/9)·21!/u^21` by splitting at 40 (the residual there is <1000, checked). V is the minimum of available bounds.

Put dθ(u)=(θ(e^u)−e^u)/e^u and Δ(u)=F(u)−u li(e^u)/e^u. For real u≥s>0,
`Δ(u)=dθ(u)+(u/s)e^(s−u)(Δ(s)−dθ(s))+u e^−u∫_s^u e^v dθ(v)/v² dv`.
This is partial summation with inclusive counts, so no half weights at the evaluation points. Treat the following branches separately (all constants directed upward):

* On u≤43.7 and u≥26, `−B(u)≤Δ(u)≤0`, with B(u)=(1.95+3.9/u+19.5/u²)e^−u/2+2u e^−u. Use PT PNT PDF p.8 and finite-RH paper §3.3 p.5. The additive allowance 2 covers li versus offset Li_2=li−li(2); indeed 0<li(2)<2 by the positive-series formula and the constant bracket above. exp(43.7)<10^19. On 26≤u≤58 also keep symmetric `c u² e^−u/2` for c=.0397888 using printed finite-RH Cor.1 PDF p.4. Both apply at the upper endpoints.
* **New signed early branch:** on 18≤v≤29.9, CH2 Lemma 9.2 supplies the ψ errors [−.751,.791] e^−v/2. The range lies within [10^4,10^13]. Dusart Prop.4.3 and Cor.4.5 give `−1.84 e^−v/2≤dθ(v)≤−.2089 e^−v/2`. Use s=18 in the identity, where Δ(18) is between −2.24e^−9 and zero by that same PT finite π quote (the series extrapolation H is not used at 18). Thus `−2.04e^−9 ≤ Δ(s)−dθ(s) ≤1.84e^−9`. For the lower Δ bound, on v≥18 the derivative of e^(v/2)/v² is at least (7/18) times the function. Hence `Δ(u)≥−[1.84(1+18/(7u))e^−u/2+2.04(u/18)e^(9−u)]` on 26≤u≤29.9. On 27≤u≤29.9 the integral is nonpositive and the anchor correction subtracts at most 1.84(u/18)e^(9−u) from the negative magnitude of dθ. Since u e^−u/2 decreases, `Δ(u)≤−.17e^−u/2` there. ## Shifted difference, full cells and tails

Form lower/upper `F_−=H+Δ_−, F_+=H+V+Δ_+` using the appropriate maximum of lower Δ bounds and minimum of upper Δ bounds. Both bounds are positive on u≥26 (the absolute losses are <1 and H≥1), so the two-argument envelope is
`L/(L−1)F_−(L−1)−F_+(L)^2 ≤ R(x)L²/x² ≤ L/(L−1)F_+(L−1)−F_−(L)^2`.
Use L≥27 for this bracket. The historical R1 remains independently valid where stated, whether or not the new bracket is sharper there. The analytic sign ray used in the main proof starts at L=27.8, which overlaps DP Lemma 3.4's [10^11,1.15·10^16] (28-ish log is well between their log endpoints; verified by exponent intervals). For [N+1,10^11] use the independent exact bridge, or the correct original integer transfer. This is the unbroken full ray, including the finite-to-analytic connection and the real left-endpoint behavior.

For quantitative testing, the deterministic portion L/(L−1)H(L−1)−H(L)^2 is bounded below by `∑_{k=4}^{20} C_k L^(−k) −∑_{k=21}^{40} S_k L^(−k)`, S_k the nonnegative square coefficients. The positively expanded shift is truncated at k=20 (all later shift terms are nonnegative). The k=0..3 coefficients vanish; C4=1, C5=14, C6=145, C7=1412, and all C4..20 are positive. Subtract the target polynomial symbolically first, then bound the rest on closed rational cells [a,b] using b for nonnegative decreasing monomials and a for negative ones. For the Δ lower (shifted) take minima of eligible positive loss upper bounds at a−1, and multiply by the safe endpoint factors b^j and a/(a−1). At the unshifted argument take e≥V(L)+Δ_+(L) by decreasing bounds at a, except the signed early gain uses V(a)−.17e^−b/2 (with lower exponential enclosure); at a cell with e<0 use a^j and H(b) for the upper bound on the negative square difference (L^j e(2H(L)+e)). With e≥0 use b^j and H(a). Check H(b)+e>0 before using square monotonicity. Branches are used only when available on the entire closed cell at their corresponding argument, thus both 58 and 59 and both 43.7 and 44.7 joins are covered without assuming the same active formula for the two arguments. There is no use of a floating point mesh as mathematical evidence. All stated rows have strict rational slack.

At a ray onset A≥191 use one constant majorant C*(A−1) for both arguments. For j≤20 the scaled errors decrease for L≥A: each L^j times the nonconstant error is bounded by terms with derivative ratios j/L−a<0 where the smallest exponential rate a is 1/8 (factor u and denominators only improve the indicated bounds, or use j+1 for the faster anchor). The factorial power u^−21 also gives decreasing scaled terms, and L/(L−1), H, V and the unscaled positive errors decrease. The negative deterministic tail magnitudes L^(j−k), k>20, decrease. Therefore the single strict check at the onset, keeping coefficient C_j to pay for the errors and discarding further positive terms, proves the stated all-ray polynomial bound. For the constant margins including 1 use j=5,z=0 after L=191. For the connected degree-2 profile first use closed cells through 191 and then the higher coefficient at 191 for the rest. All these are checks of inequalities on bins and analytic tails, not a global optimization.

## Limitations and attempts

The finite π branches were re-derived at the trust of the actual original quotes, not the sharper single-log project-interface stub. The early signed branch reduces error by its π square sign rather than adding a false unconditional lower bound on |π−li|. Using the all-ray q part alone leaves a substantial late-L loss after multiplying by L^6 on the degree-2 profile; the initial attempt at +10/L² across 48–191 failed around 189–190. With the improved bound there remains a dip below the +10 sufficient comparison around L=189.13. The integrated remainder and refined density move that transition and repair the connected profile without making the stronger +10 claim across it. Local coefficients such as +100 need later left or earlier right endpoints; [50,169] failed the sufficient test. Lowering the final sign threshold is impossible because of the exact local zero and negative values just left. Reducing its reliance on literature for the remaining large finite part would require independently replaying DP Lemma 3.4 or extending the new prime-count bridge; neither is claimed.
