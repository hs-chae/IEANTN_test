# D02 Mertens and prime-sum envelope

Let $P=\prod_{p\le x}(1-1/p), Q=1/P$ and use the real
convention $p\le x$. Write
$$
 \delta_Q=\log Q-\gamma-\log L,\quad
 \delta_S=\sum_{p\le x}1/p-\log L-M,\quad
 \delta_V=\sum_{p\le x}(\log p)/p-L+B_3 .
$$
Here $M=\gamma+\sum_p[\log(1-1/p)+1/p]$ and
$B_3=\gamma+\sum_p(\log p)/(p(p-1))$, exactly as in
Dusart Thms. 5.6–5.7 (PDF pp.17–18). Define the common error
quantity
$$
 \mathcal E(x)=\max\{L|e^{-\delta_Q}-1|, L|e^{\delta_Q}-1|,
                            L|\delta_S|, |\delta_V|\}.
$$
The two signed product factors are $e^{-\delta_Q}$ and
$e^{\delta_Q}$; the maximum of the two unscaled relative
errors is exactly $e^{|\delta_Q|}-1$.

## 1. Exact identities and signed refinement at every point

Let $K(v)=(1+v)/v^2$, $D_1=\sum_{p^k>x,k\ge2}\log p/p^k$,
$D_0=\sum_{p^k>x,k\ge2}1/(kp^k)$,
$\tau=\sum_{p>x,k\ge2}1/(kp^k)$, and $D_Q=D_0-\tau$.
The exact real identities, including at jumps, are
$$
 \delta_V=e_{\psi}(L)-J_1(L)+D_1,\quad
 \delta_S=e_{\psi}(L)/L-\int_L^\infty e_{\psi}(v)K(v)dv+D_0,\quad
 \delta_Q=\delta_S-\tau .
$$
They follow by Abel summation of
$\sum\Lambda(n)/n$ and $\sum\Lambda(n)/(n\log n)$,
subtracting constants at infinity and adding the omitted prime
powers; details in `mertens_current.md` §2. For example the
normalization $M$ identifies the $U_0$ limit as $\gamma$
because the full correction beyond all prime powers is absolutely
summable, and the $U_1$ limit is $-B_3+(B_3-\gamma)$.
No equality assumes the RH. The same method from $\theta$
instead of $\psi$ gives the alternative direct signed
integral envelope in `mertens_current.md` §4, also allowing
the D02 theta curve under the integral.

By two integrations by parts and $J_2'=-J_1$,
$$
 J_K(L)=K(L)j(L)+2|K'(L)|j_2(L)
        \ge  \left|\int_L^\infty e_{\psi}(v)K(v)dv\right|.
$$
Indeed the integral is $K(L)J_1(L)+\int_L^\infty J_1K'$;
the last integral is $J_2(L)K'(L)+\int_L^\infty J_2K''$.
Here $K''>0$ and $\int_L^\infty K''=|K'(L)|$.
All boundary limits hold by boundedness of the primitives.
Use $j,j_2$ of D02.md §3. This improves the simple
inherited smoothed off-line factor $e^{-L/8}$ on the
**whole** domain $L\ge18.42$, even when the boundary
$\psi(x)$ is not improved by A. The conditional exponent
is applied to an absolutely convergent series, not to an
uncertified extrapolation of a finite tail. A known boundary
value $e_{\psi}(L)$ can be kept exactly, with a strict-tail
correction interval rather than replacing it by a worst-case error.

For $L\ge18.42$, choose both signs of $e_{\psi}(L)$ as in
`mertens_current.md` §2, taking their separate minima
also with $e^{-aL}C(L)$ when $L\ge155$ and with the
contract on its actual direct domain. Call the signed
interval $[-E_-,E_+]$. For $\delta_V$ add the $D_1$
interval to $[-E_- - j,E_++j]$; for $\delta_S,\delta_Q$
add the $D_0,D_Q$ intervals to
$[-E_-/L-J_K,E_+/L+J_K]$.
The $D_i$ intervals in that section apply unchanged
($0\le D_1\le2e^{-L/2}, 0\le D_Q\le D_0\le2 e^{-L/2}/L$
are sufficient over the high bridge). For a certified
$[l,u]$ for $\delta_Q$, the product intervals are
$[e^l,e^u]$ for $Q$'s normalized factor and
$[e^{-u},e^{-l}]$ for $P$'s. Take the best separate
endpoints, not only the common-error tables. The finite
product and signed prime-sum branches retain their own
better endpoints where applicable.

**Optional sharper elementary correction brackets.** The
following operations can be added on the same domain without
new analytic hypotheses. For a nonnegative decreasing
differentiable $f$ with $tf(t)\to0$, let
$I_f(y)=\int_y^\infty f(t)dt$. If
$|\theta(t)/t-1|\le d(y)$ for $t\ge y$, then
$$
 \left|\sum_{p>y}\log p\, f(p)-I_f(y)\right|
 \le d(y)(2yf(y)+I_f(y)).
$$
This is exact partial summation at the inclusive $\theta(y)$;
bound $\int_y^\infty t(-f'(t))dt=yf(y)+I_f(y)$.
For each $k=2,3$ take $y=x^{1/k}$,
$f(t)=t^{-k}$ for $D_1$, and $f(t)=1/(kt^k\log t)$
for $D_0$; the latter integral is
$\frac1k\int_{(1-1/k)L}^\infty e^{-w}dw/w$.
For $\tau$ take $y=x$ and $k=2,3$ in the latter kernel.
Use $d(y)=A_0+117/\sqrt y$ or, where applicable, any
decreasing majorant from D02.md §3 and the inherited profiles,
and intersect the resulting intervals.
For the unsummed $k\ge4$ part of $D_1$, upper-bound by
$$
 \frac{2e^{-3L/4}}{\log 2}
       \left(\frac{4L}{3}+\frac{16}{9}\right).
$$
In fact $\sum_{4\le k\le v/\log2}\theta(e^{v/k})
\le2(v/\log2)e^{v/4}$ by Dusart Thm. 4.2 $(k=0)$,
and dropping the negative boundary in Abel summation gives
the integral of this bound times $e^{-v}$.
Divide by $L$ for $D_0$ or as a subset bound for $\tau$.
For $\tau$ the even smaller elementary bound for $k\ge4$
is $\sum_{n>\lfloor x\rfloor}1/[4n^3(n-1)]$.
Use zero as lower bound for these remaining positive tails.
These corrections improve directional accuracy independently
of the main spectral term. D02 enters them only when the
theta bound at the relevant *root* is improved, not by using
the unrooted onset improperly.

## 2. Combine poles before bounding the power-error branch

There is a slightly stronger symmetric spectral bound than
adding $C e^{-aL}$ and the absolute integral independently.
With $T=e^{c_* L}$ (either licensed cutoff in D02.md), the $|\gamma|\le T^*$ terms of
$e_{\psi}-J_1$ from the two formulas in D02.md combine **exactly**
to $-e^{(\rho-1)L}/(\rho-1)$. Its absolute denominator
is still at least $|\gamma|$, so the *same* screen bounds
the sum. Only the smoothed high tail, not the unsmoothed
one, must be added. By CH2 Cor. B.2 and the $\beta \le b$
cap its relative size is at most $e^{-aL}\eta(L)$ with
$\eta(L)=2(c_*L+1)e^{-c_*L}/\pi$ (integrate the count over
$t\ge T$ against $4/t^3$ and drop $-\log(2\pi)$).
The trivial terms in $J_1$ add at most $2e^{-L}$, and the
FKS truncation term has already been paid in $C$.
For the minimum screen at cap $b$ use $c_*=.27$ in $\eta$
regardless of which branch attained the minimum: its tail
allowance is at least the one for .35. Put $U=C+\eta+2e^{-bL}$ and $J=e^{aL}j, J'=e^{aL}j_2$
(the prime on this last symbol is just a label).
Then $|\delta_V-D_1|\le e^{-aL}U$, and each of
$|\delta_S-D_0|, |\delta_Q-D_Q|$ is bounded by
$$
 \frac{e^{-aL}}{L}
 \left[U+\frac{J}{L}+2(1/L+2/L^2)J'\right].
$$
For the latter subtract $e_{\psi}-J_1$ from $e_{\psi}-L\int e_{\psi} K$;
the remaining terms are $-J_1/L-L\int J_1K'$, as bounded above.
This joint bound and the separate signed intervals need not
dominate each other for every endpoint. Retain their intersection.

One convenient common ray uses
$$
 z=U+J/L+2(1/L+2/L^2)J' +2e^{-(1/2-a)L}+Le^{-bL},\qquad
 Z=\frac{z}{1-ze^{-aL}/L}.
$$
Then $\mathcal E(x)\le e^{-aL}Z(L)$ for $L\ge155$.
The last positive term is optional (it upper-allows the
$\tau$ correction even when the tight $D_Q$ interval
isn't used). The prime-power bounds suffice on this entire
domain. The denominator is positive at 155, and the
individual $z$-terms and $e^{-aL}/L$ decrease; the
bound follows from $e^v-1\le v/(1-v)$ for $0\le v<1$.
This proves the limiting allowance $w_b<.116297$, without
even the residual reciprocal-square coefficient from the
separate summands. The no-FKS-table alternative in the table
uses the easier separate bounds, with the unrefined $k$
and $J'\le J$.

**Boundary-zero obstruction (not existence).** Termwise
bounded convergence in the *absolutely* summable high
strip, with FKS's signed formula and growing cutoff,
gives the leading possible spectral term
$$
 e^{aL}e_{\psi}(L)=-2\Re \sum_{\beta=b,\,\gamma>0}e^{i\gamma L}/\rho+o(1).
$$
Indeed the density screen is summable at $s_*$; each term
with $\beta<b$ goes to zero after scaling, and low-strip
and truncation losses go to zero. Smoothed transfer or the
combined-pole formula shows that the corresponding leading
term for $e^{aL}\delta_V, Le^{aL}\delta_S, Le^{aL}\delta_Q$
has denominator $\rho-1$ instead, and the products have
opposite signs after linearization. This does not assert any
boundary zeros. A further logarithmic saving at the same
power (little-o of the current shape) would exclude them:
reverse partial summation on $U_1=\sum Lambda(n)/n$ or
$U_0=\sum Lambda(n)/(n\log n)$, adding the smaller prime-power
corrections, to get $\psi(x)-x=o(x^b)$.
For $U_1$ integrate against $t$, and for $U_0$ against
$t\log t$; the main derivatives give $x+O(1)$, and
the boundary and integrated little-o terms are both
$o(x^b)$. For products the logarithm preserves little-o
near 1. At $s=\rho+\epsilon$ in the Mellin formula of D02.md
the integral times $\epsilon$ then tends to 0, contradicting
a logarithmic-derivative pole at a zero with $\Re\rho=b$.
A fixed gain in exponent with any fixed log power would
force a smaller zero strip by the same continuation argument.

The initially attempted use of the raw contract and
an absolute integration tail overpays both $L^2/64$
and $1/a$; it does not yield the present transition
or limiting constants. Density screening, a finer
boundary chord, independent reciprocal moments, and
combining the poles resolve those losses. A more aggressive
old-shape coefficient below .00132 fails the current
bridge test around $L=167.65$; this is not a violation
of the underlying prime estimates. The exact small
benchmark onsets cannot improve, and other small-argument
gains are limited by the finite-state prime tapes, not
by A evaluated outside its domain. The finer tape is
substantive but does not prove globally optimal finite
cutoffs. No unknown sign or boundary zero is assumed.
