## Error transfer and pointwise envelope

Use PV $\operatorname{li}$. The error in using
$\operatorname{Li}_2(x)=\int_2^x dt/\log t$ is the constant
$\operatorname{li}(2)\in(0,4)$, as proved in `ramanujan.md`
§2. With $F(t)=t e^{-t}\operatorname{li}(e^t)$, partial
summation gives the exact boundary-plus-integral decomposition
$$
\pi(e^t)-\operatorname{li}(e^t)
=\frac{\theta(e^t)-e^t}{t}
+\int_{\log2}^t \frac{e^v}{v^2}\left(\theta(e^v)e^{-v}-1\right)dv
+2/\log2-\operatorname{li}(2).
$$
The same formula governs both $x$ and $x/e$, not a bound
for the shifted argument inferred at $x$.

Write $D(t)$ for a two-sided bound for
$|t e^{-t}\pi(e^t)-F(t)|$; take the minimum of every applicable
inherited entry in `ramanujan.md` §2. Add from $t\ge155$:

$$

Also include $A_0+117e^{-t/2}+H_s(t)$ from 58, or

## Polynomial approximation and proof for the whole domain

For integer $N\ge17$ in the finite certificates, put
$P_N(t)=\sum_{j=0}^N j!/t^j$ and
$f_N(t)=\sum_{j=0}^N(1-j/t)^{-1}$ for $t>N+1$.
Use
$$
P_N(t)-f_N(t)e^{1-t} < F(t) < P_N(t)+d_N(t)
$$
with
$$
d_N(t)=4t e^{-t}+
\min\big((N+1)!/t^N, 2(N+1)!/t^{N+1}+t e^{-t/2}\big).
$$
To verify, substitute $v=t-\log u$ in $\int_e^{e^t}du/\log u$.
The finite geometric sum through degree $N$ has positive
remainder $(v/t)^{N+1}/(1-v/t)$. Its integral over
$0\le v\le t-1$ can be bounded by $t$ times the full
moment, or by splitting at $t/2$ and bounding the two
pieces with 2 and $t$ respectively. Missing moment
tails are bounded using $(1+w/t)^j\le e^{jw/t}$ after
$v=t-1+w$. Also $0<\operatorname{li}(e)<4$.
This accounts for the direction of both errors.

Expand $L P_N(L-1)/(L-1)$ in powers of $1/L$ with
positive binomial coefficients. Through degree $N$ the
difference from $P_N(L)^2$ has coefficients $a_n$.
For $n\ge6$, $a_n/n!\ge 65/24-(2+4/n)>1/24$:
bound the two end binomial reciprocals by 1 each, the
next two by $1/n$, and all other $n-3$ entries by
$2/[n(n-1)]$; lower bound the factorial reciprocal sum
through degree 4. Directly check the small $a_n$.
The sign argument applies also at degrees beyond the
computational tables.

The checker subtracts the positive tails explicitly. In
unnormalized units for $G/L^4$, define the main error allowance
$$
 E_N=(2P_N+d_N)d_N+\frac{L}{L-1} f_N(L-1)e^{2-L}
 +\sum_{0\le j,k\le N, j+k>N}\frac{j!k!}{L^{j+k}} .
$$
The count-error allowance is
$$
 E_{\pi}=\frac{L}{L-1}D_-(L-1)+(2[P_N(L)+d_N(L)]+D_+(L))D_+(L).
$$
Thus $G(L)>\sum_{j=4}^N a_j/L^{j-4}-L^4(E_N+E_\pi)$.
For a profile missing the term of degree $n$ first,
the lower reserve at scale $L^{n-4}$ uses powers
$a_j/L^{j-n}$, $j=n,\ldots,N$.
On a closed cell $[l,r]$ every nonnegative decreasing
error factor is bounded at $l$, and the multiplying power
$L^n$ and lower polynomial at $r$. Domain-dependent bounds
are used only when they hold on the whole argument interval.
In particular at $x/e$ the bounds are selected with
$[l-1,r-1]$, not just the starting argument.

For the integer-log tail table and the fixed $K$ tails a
stronger single-check argument **keeps all the excess main
coefficients**: after multiplying by $L^N$, the reserve
polynomial is nondecreasing, since all coefficients of the
remaining $a_j$ (or $a_6-K$) are nonnegative. The scaled
total errors decrease on the tail: powers of $1/L$ and
other rational prefactors decrease; $L^N e^{-c(L-h)}$
decreases for $L>N/c$, and with an extra factor $(L-h)^i$
use $N/L+i/(L-h)<c$. For the screened error factor use the
monotone $C$ and $c=a$ or $1/8$ (no growing positive
power multiplies that term except $L^N$); standalone
terms with $i=1$ have faster decay. These inequalities
hold at every checked tail. The direct-A variant has $i\le2$
at rate $a$ and starts its tail at 222. In $E_N$, the slowest
pure inverse power of the remainder has degree $N$, and
the fastest scale of the exponential factors is $L^{N+1}$
at decay at least $1/2$. The minimum of the two remainder
branches after positive scaling is nonincreasing. This proves
every infinite tail rather than extrapolating the samples.

For the shifted claim $-\log(1-s)\le2s<4/L^4<.001$
at and beyond 58. Let $v=1+4/L^4$; then
$\log y\ge L-v$. Moving the *li* main term from $x/e$ to
$y$ costs at most $(x/e-y)/\log y$. In units $G$ its
cost is $(1+13/L+68/L^2)/(1-v/L)$.
Subtract $1+14/L$ from this cost and multiply by $L^2$
to get $(68+14v+4/L^3)/(1-v/L)$, decreasing. The count
error is bounded separately at $\log y\ge L-1.001$;
the prefactor is at most $L/(L-1.001)$, since $y/(x/e)\le1$.
The true log argument lies in $[l-1.001,r-1]$ on a cell
and `d02ram.py` uses these domains. Use the error allowances
at scale $L^6$; retain positive main powers on cells and
only $a_6$ for the monotonically decreasing-error tail at 172.
The 68-shift strict lower reserve exceeds 1 on $[58,167]$
and the tail from 172, and .74 on the closed cells joining
them. Change 68 to 67.7 in the cost on those same cells to
get a lower reserve exceeding 1; outside that transition the
smaller shrink inherits the strict target by monotonicity
of the real prime count. Alternatively the reserve test is
even stronger there with the smaller cost. For the 12-only
shift use its **own** cost and target. After cancelling the
first two leading main terms, the sum of its li cost and its
target at scale $L^6$ subtracts
$$
69+(13v+4/L^3)/(1-v/L)
$$
from the second-order reserve, a decreasing quantity.
The same cells and tail at 172 pass that exact test.
No comparison of individual primes between the two arguments
is required. For the asymptotic limitation, with general
fixed $s=(1+\alpha/L+\beta/L^2)/L^4$, the correlated
li cost equals $1+(1+\alpha)/L+(1+\alpha+\beta)/L^2+
O(L^{-3})$ by integration over the shrinking log interval;
the count errors are $o(L^{-m})$ for every fixed $m$.
Subtract this from $1+14/L+145/L^2+O(L^{-3})$.

## Arbitrarily high orders: uniform remainder estimate

For $L\ge26n, L\ge192, n\ge6$, use $N=n+3$,
the split remainder, and the actual screen at $L,L-1$.
We have $P_N\le1+2/L$ from the decreasing ratios of
successive terms, $P_N+d_N<1.02$, and $f_N(L-1)\le2(n+4)$.
Use $(n+4)/L\le1/16$. The convolution terms of degree $j>N$
from the square have coefficient at most $3j!$ by the
reciprocal binomial bound. Since $(2n+6)/L\le 3/26$,
geometric summation and the split remainder give
$$
 E_N\le 10(n+4)!/L^{n+4}+5Le^{-L/2}.
$$
For example the pure factorial coefficient is bounded by
$3/(1-3/26)+6<10$; the other remainders use
$2P_N+d_N<3$, and the missing shifted moment is bounded
by $4(n+4)e^{2-L}$.
The count allowance is at most $.79e^{-aL}$ using
$D_2(t)<.24e^{-at}$ at and after 180 (in this proof only $t\ge191$ is needed), and at most
$.47e^{-aL}$ using $D_2(t)<.144e^{-at}$ from 535.
The two uniform $D_2$ caps follow by multiplying by
$e^{at}$ termwise in the positive decreasing envelope
and checking the starts. The prefactor in the shifted loss
is at most $192e^a/191$; all other multipliers are
included in the numeric guards in `d02ram.py`.

For $L\ge32n$ bound the three errors after dividing by
$a_n/L^n$ using $a_n>n!/24$, $n!\ge(n/e)^n$;
the total is at most
$$
24\big(.79(32e^{1-32a})^n+5\cdot32n(32e^{-15})^n+10/16^4\big)<1.
$$
Check at $n=6$; the decays are monotone. For $n\ge20$
use $a_n>n!/2$ and
$n!\ge \sqrt{2n}(n/e)^n$, the latter from integrating
$\log t$ over the unit intervals centered at $1,ldots,n$
and using concavity. Put $c=27.6443$,
$L_0=cn-4\log n-2$; then $L_0\ge26n, L_0>536$.
The logarithmic inequalities $\log c+1-ac<0$ and
$4(a-1/c)<1/2$, by $\log(1-z)\le-z$, imply
$$
 L_0^n e^{-aL_0}/(n/e)^n
 \le \sqrt n\,e^{2(a-1/c)}.
$$
The monotone count term is at most
$.94 e^{2(a-1/c)}/\sqrt2$ relative to $a_n/L^n$.
Bound the other two terms by $20/16^4$ and
$10\cdot26 n(26e^{-12})^n$ and check their sum with
the count term at 20. This establishes the arbitrarily
high-order result without evaluating huge factorial intervals
at arbitrarily large $n$.

## Limits and attempted improvements

The sharp sign benchmark cannot be advanced. Extending a
substantial **normalized** margin down to its vicinity would
require finer finite prime counts or a sharper finite analytic
count envelope; the original integer check alone supplies no
uniform integer margin. The early one-sided certificate still
fails at $L=27.7$ (the sufficient reserve is negative,
**not** an actual negative gap). The larger $K=90$ coefficient
does not fill the entire CH2-to-D02 transition at the precision
of the endpoint error bounds; use the validated earlier bounded
piece, $K=83$ and later 90 ray instead. Direct substitution
of the contract remains useful without density but does not
give the early second-coefficient transition: its large
$L^2/64$ coefficient consumes that reserve. The proof addresses
that bottleneck via the strip, density, and smoothed prefix.
The QRH-free and D01 pieces in the inherited portfolio keep
their actual premise and trust tiers regardless of these trials.
