## Analytic derivation
Let d(t)=Dθ(t) and E_i be the centered prime sum errors, i=1 reciprocal (subtract log L+M), i=2 log-weighted (subtract L−B3). Dusart Thms 5.6–7 give the constants and tail identities
E1 = d(L)/L−∫_L^∞ d(t)(1/t+1/t²)dt, E2=d(L)−∫_L^∞d(t)dt.
Also E1=E2/L−∫_L^∞E2(e^t)dt/t² (here E2 on x-scale); this is partial summation of the weighted sum with 1/log p. Put J=ψ−θ. By CH2 Cor 1.3, |E2|≤D+113.67 e^(−L/2)+∫_(x,∞)dJ/u. The last term equals −J(x)/x+∫_x^∞J(u)/u² du, less than 1.004 e^(−L/2) for L≥40 by Dusart Prop 4.3 and Cor 4.5 (PDF pp.12–13) and the check in tails.py. This yields the moderate branch by the E1 identity. Further, the centered log product is Z=log Q−γ−log L=E1−∑_{p>x}∑_{k≥2}1/(kp^k). The last nonnegative correction is ≤1/(2 floor x) ≤1/x, using ∑_{k≥2}1/(kp^k)≤1/[2p(p−1)]. Thus both reciprocal relative errors are ≤exp(M1+e^−L)−1.
For the range 21≤L≤40, PT PDF p.8 gives |π(u)−Li_2(u)|≤2.2√u/log u+2 (as in candidate.md). Apply integration by parts over [x,e^40] to E1 with test function 1/u, and to E2 with log(u)/u, taking the already bounded error at the right endpoint. For the reciprocal sum the resulting losses are ≤6.6e^(−L/2)/L+4e^−L plus error at e^40 and the right endpoint π-error times 1/e^40. The right endpoint contributions sum to <7e-9 by tails.py. For E2 the losses are ≤6.6e^(−L/2)+4L e^−L plus the right endpoint combined <3e-7, using ∫_L^∞(t−1)e^−t dt=L e^−L. e^21<B0 follows; e^25<B also follows from the same certificate. Scaled comparison uses monotonicity of L^k e^−aL on rays after k/a; for e^s−1 use s/(1−s) at small s.

## Oscillatory tail bound
For L≥190 and any positive differentiable nonincreasing w, |∫_L^∞d(t)w(t)dt|≤w(L) e^−(L/8) K(L), with K in tails.py. To prove this, hold T=e^(L/2−2) fixed on the entire interval [L,3L]. FKS Thm 3.2 applies (51,3L<T; T<(e^(L/2)−2)/5). Use only its zeros with |γ|≤T as a common finite sum. Deleting the remaining zeros up to T*(t)≤2.45T changes the pointwise error by at most their absolute sum. Bound both-sign reciprocal-zero weight at ≤2.45T by E=(L/2)^2/6+2 as in candidate.md. For deleted low zeros β<.70 use E exp(−.30t) and integrate to ∞; their loss after factoring w(L)e^(−L/8) is (10/3)E exp(−.175 L). For deleted higher zeros use bands s,t' from .70,.75,.80,.85,.875; they contribute ≤V_s exp((t'−1)t). V_s is formula (P0) with log H replaced by z=log T and q rounded up to 4 (valid z≥93). Integrate each band to infinity giving V_s exp((t'−.875)L)/(1−t'). Finite cutoffs may occur at actual ordinates; keep ≤T and bound >T only, so all endpoints work.
For the common finite sum, integration by parts gives |∫_L^(3L) exp((ρ−1)t)w(t)dt|≤2 w(L) exp((β−1)L)/|1−ρ| (use w(3L)+∫|w'|=w(L)). For β<.70 use |1−ρ|≥.30, giving (20/3)E exp(−.175L); for β≥.70 use |1−ρ|≥γ>H and the whole high-zero part of F(L), so loss ≤2F(L)/H. The FKS error integrates to ≤8.364 L² exp(2−3L/8) in this normalization. The unexpanded tail t>3L costs at most 8P exp(−L/4) by the pure power bound; the correction from ψ to θ on [L,3L] costs ≤2.01 exp(−3L/8) by Dusart Cor 4.5. These are exactly the terms in tails.py. They all decrease for L≥190 by logarithmic derivatives (z^4e^−rz/(r−4/z) decreases there). The exact estimates give K(190)<8e-9, K(400)<9.1e-15.
For the claimed far ray insert |d(L)|≤Cθ(L) e^−L/8, Cθ(L)=F(L)+1.000000147e^−3L/8+1.78e^−13L/24, into E1,E2. This gives exact coefficient functions Cθ+(1+1/L)K for E1 and Cθ+K for E2. Add e^−L for the product's log then exponentiate with s/(1−s). The useful closed constants at L=190 and 400 are certified in tails.py using decreasing coefficients on the entire respective rays.

## Finite certificate proof (ordinary exact integer computation)
sieve.c marks all and only composites in contiguous segments using all primes up to √B, generated by Eratosthenes. All series are evaluated as 128-bit integer rational enclosures at scale S=2^58. To bound S log n, reduce n by a power 2^k to [1,2), then use 2 atanh((n−2^k)/(n+2^k)). For z in [0,1/3] the scaled floor of z, squared then floored, gives a lower integer recurrence for successive odd powers; error <4 units since the recurrence contracts by at least 1/9 and the squared input error is <2 units. Dividing terms adds <1 unit per term; 32 terms and the geometric residual give total <128 units for a single atanh logarithm. Thus ln0(n) has lower error <128(k+1). ll0 uses ln0(ln0(n))−58(lg2+128); propagation of initial log error for n≥1000 costs ≤4864/6 units and the two log computations <16000 more, hence lower enclosure width 18000 as used. For gamma use H_n−log n−1/(2n)≤γ≤H_n−log n−1/(2n)+1/n². This follows by summing the trapezoid excess for 1/t over [k,k+1] from k=n onward; excess is nonnegative and at most sup|(1/t)''|/12≤1/(6k³), whose sum is <1/n². Floors are directed.
The logQ series −log(1−1/p)=∑_{k≥1}1/(kp^k) uses floors until the first subunit, whose remaining tail is <2 units by successive ratio ≤1/2. Terms after k=1 are also the correction defining M=γ−sum_{p,k≥2}1/(kp^k). B3=γ+sum log p/[p(p−1)]. For constants compute corrections through p≤M0=10^7. The original Dusart Thm. 4.2 (PDF p.11, k=2, η₂=.2 from x≥3594641) implies |θ(u)−u|≤.001u for u≥M0. Then partial summation with 1/u² bounds \(\sum_{p>M0}\log p/p²\) between .997/M0 and 1.003/M0. Replacing 1/p² by 1/[p(p−1)] adds at most 1.003/M0², so the C tail interval [.997/M0,1.004/M0] is rigorous. For the other constant, partial summation with 1/(u² log u) bounds the difference between \(\sum_{p>M0}1/p²\) and I=∫_(M0)^∞du/(u² log u) by .001(3+1/l)/(M0 l) where l=log M0. Integration by parts gives (1/l−1/l²)/M0≤I≤(1/l−1/l²+2/l³)/M0. Half of this integral is the main tail; terms of order p^−3 and higher add ≤1/(2 M0²) by comparison with all integers and telescoping. Using 16<l<16.2 (checked in tails.py) gives the enclosing interval [2.8e−9,3.1e−9]. Earlier elementary bounds (also valid and used for the small x verification) were 1/M0 and (log(M0+1)+1)/M0 by comparison with all integers and telescoping.
For E2, log p/p is evaluated directly through p≤10000. In each later block (a,b], (log a−1)/p+1/a encloses log p/p from above, with error at most (b−a)^2/(2a²p) by 0≤z−log(1+z)≤z²/2. The block uses the interval sum of 1/p and exact count of primes; integer truncation <2 units is covered. Partial sums of all three quantities are nondecreasing. Between successive block ends a and b they lie between the lower sum at a and the upper sum at b, while log x and log log x also increase. Thus no interior prime or real is missed. The error budget is lower bounded by evaluating the decreasing target at b using an integer ceiling of the logarithm scaled by 1024 and (in sqrt mode) an upper integer sqrt. For products, an error radius q is certified by requiring |Z|<q/(1+q), so exp(|Z|)−1≤|Z|/(1−|Z|)<q. All integer bounds fit 128 bits; even unscaled n is at most 7.5·10^10 and all scaled logarithms are <2^65. No libm, prime table, or external numerical inputs enter.
