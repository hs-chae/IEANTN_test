Exposure experiments: what worked, what did not, and where to stop

The experiments demonstrate some useful proof reuse and theorem construction. They do not establish an overall exposure advantage, a discovery advantage, or source-first program verification by exposed workers.

Pause new exposure research now; package the evidence.

Another open-ended experiment round is not justified by the current signal. Preserve the mixed and negative outcomes, publish an honest descriptive report, and separate this question from completing the mathematical software already built.

Design limitations and the shared packet defect

The supplied exact-real definitions lacked a noncomputable annotation. This was identified after all six submissions had been sealed. Workers handled the stubs differently; corresponding defects were shared across arms. Original submissions were not silently repaired and rescored, and there were no selective retries or post-outcome budget changes.

There is one run per task/arm, only three tasks, and no controlled provider generation RNG seed. This is a descriptive format comparison, not a powered confirmatory study. Formal completion, discovery of a better result, and source-first verification are distinct outcomes.

How the multi-day effort was spent

These six worker sessions used 36.41 total worker minutes and 69,958 generated tokens. Independent checks used 14.31 total minutes. The several-day campaign also includes separate C++ source proofs, mathematical refinements, compilation, reproducibility and integration work. It would be misleading to attribute all that elapsed work to exposure discovery.

Later coordinator proof completion is supplemental mathematical work. It does not turn the workers’ 0/6 source-first-PV count into a positive experimental result.

3. What the retrospective and intervention evidence adds

Trace audit: concrete reuse, limited causal evidence

The retrospective audit covered all 18 original historical traces: 3,113 byte-addressed public events and 281 completed command executions. It also examined all 32 original T01 traces in the main study. Four successful formal-exposure T01 sessions adapted quotient, valuation and divisor-sum proof bodies and added new multiplicity/loss estimates. Other sessions reused bodies without closing the core gap, or mainly cited and packaged them.

Packets were provided inline in the first message. That establishes availability, not attention or causation. The record contains commands, drafts, edits and final responses, not unrecorded private reasoning. The audit did not assign its strongest causal grades.

Counterfactual controls: removing the selected input did not prevent success

The corrected broad and narrowly targeted control cohorts each contained 24 sessions. Original mathematical support was 46/48; 45/48 were both locked and supported. Later written/computational completion brought a separate posthoc total to 48/48. These controls produced zero new Lean/network completions in that study. An earlier invalid 12-run fidelity cohort was retained and excluded.

Full exposure did not consistently dominate removed-input or prose controls. Residual consumers and shared sources allowed reconstruction even after selected material was removed. Strong ordinary Ramanujan onset improvements also occurred in removed-critical controls. These observations oppose a claim that the exposed structure was necessary; they do not prove that every possible exposure design is ineffective.

Blinded reviews were model reviews, not independent human expert adjudication. Historical native/source-conditional evidence is not relabelled as source-first PV.

Recorded completed build: 14,878 modules, comprising 10,970 explicitly validated source-built parent modules and 3,908 fresh compilations. All 20,541 selected typed roots passed with only the standard three axioms. This is a validated chain, not another empty-application-bank run.
