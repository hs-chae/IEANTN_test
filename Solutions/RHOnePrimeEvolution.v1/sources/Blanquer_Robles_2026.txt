          INTERVALS, AND ARITHMETIC PROGRESSIONS


                         RAFAEL BLANQUER ROBLES


       Abstract. Assuming the Riemann hypothesis, the limit superior of consecutive prime gaps
        normalized by √pn log pn is less than 0.834051. Under the generalized Riemann hypothesis, for
        each fixed ϵ > 0 and all sufficiently large x, every reduced class modulo 1 ≤q ≤√x/(log x)1+ϵ
         contains a prime in (x, x+0.851128 φ(q)√x log x]. At coefficient 0.85125 the same classes contain
         at least 8.165 · 10−5√x primes in the interval. A separate Fourier witness gives, under the
        ordinary Riemann hypothesis, a prime in (x, x + 0.85125√x log x] for every real x ≥4.  For
       x ≥4·1018 we give an explicit lower bound for the number of primes in the latter interval using
        a level-set bound for the exterior primes. Rational dual step functions and primal witnesses
         enclose each of 1/C+(36/11) and 1/C+(4) in an interval of width less than 0.007.



                                   1. Introduction and results

  Write pn for the nth prime and π(x) for the number of primes p ≤x. Let π(x; q, b) count those
with p ≡b (mod q). All logarithms are natural. RH denotes the Riemann hypothesis for ζ(s),
and GRH includes RH and the corresponding hypotheses for primitive Dirichlet L-functions.
  Carneiro, Milinovich and Soundararajan [2] bound normalized prime gaps using an extremal
problem in Fourier analysis. Chirre, Pereira Júnior and de Laat [3] obtained a bound of 0.8358
for the ordinary prime-gap limit superior and a coefficient of 0.8531 for intervals in a reduced
residue class with fixed modulus. Rational test functions on a compact Fourier support improve
these coefficients.  Grenié, Molteni and Perelli [7, Theorem 1] proved a uniform estimate at
scale φ(q)√x log x with absolute constants. Dudek, Grenié and Molteni [8, Theorem 1.1 and
Table 1] give explicit symmetric intervals for every q ≥3.  For example, one row gives half-
width φ(q)(0.7 log y + 5)√y about y when y ≥(200φ(q) log q)2. In general their half-width is
φ(q)(α0 log y + δ0 log q + ρ0)√y with a numerical threshold. Re-centering an interval of length
L at y = x + L/2 gives leading coefficient 2α0 + 2δ0 log q/ log x for the full length in the family
(1), where L = o(x) and q ≪√x. The row with (α0, δ0) = (1/2, 1/3) gives leading coefficient at
most 4/3 + o(1) in this family, tending to 1 for fixed q. Theorem 2 gives a smaller asymptotic
coefficient in that family and an interval-length bound for a specified lower prime density. The
proof retains the conductor dependence in Guinand–Weil and scales the Brun–Titchmarsh blocks
with the modulus.
