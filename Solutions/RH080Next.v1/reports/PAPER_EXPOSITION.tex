\]

\section{Fourier witnesses and the actual analytic transfer}
Use the convention
\[
 \widehat F(v)=\int_{\RR}F(u)e^{-2\pi iuv}\,du,\quad
 J(F)=\int_{|v|>1}\max(0,\widehat F(v))\,dv,\quad
 D_A(F)=F(0)-AJ(F).
\]
The CMS class consists of real even continuous nonzero \(L^1\) functions,
with genuine real Fourier transform, finite positive-part leakage, and
\(D_A(F)>0\) when a quotient is used. Integrability of the whole Fourier
transform is not imposed. Set \(Q_A(F)=\norm F_1/D_A(F)\).

For positive integer mesh \(m\), rational nodes with terminal value zero define
an even affine window \(W\). With \(N=5m\), BR Lemma~5 states
\[
 \begin{split}
 P(t)&=a_0+2\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}a_j\cos(2\pi jt),\\
 F(u)&=m^{-1}\operatorname{sinc}^2(u/m)P(u/m),\\
 \norm F_1&=\int_0^1|P(t)|\,dt,
 \end{split}\tag{4}\label{eq:BRpair}
\]
where \(\operatorname{sinc}(t)=\sin(\pi t)/(\pi t)\) with its removable value
at zero \cite[Lemma~5, Eq.~(4), p.~3]{BR}.
The norm identity remains a named general literature input. The actual affine
Fourier correspondence, integrability and nonzero eligibility are separately
kernel-proved. The support-seven witness uses mesh \(360\); a proved mesh
dilation to \(504\) places the same nodal data in the literal support-five
source class. No new support-seven source axiom or normalization \(F(0)=1\)
is introduced.

BR Proposition~6 gives the source population inequality
\[
 \frac{\pi(x+c\varphi(q)H(x);q,b)-\pi(x;q,b)}{\sqrt{x}}
 \ge
 \frac{cD_4(F)-\norm F_1}{2M_W}
 -O_{F,c}\!\left(
 \eta(x)+\frac{\log\log x}{\log x}+\frac{\log x}{\sqrt{x}}\right),
 \tag{5}\label{eq:BRcount}
\]
where \(M_W=\norm W_\infty\), under its modulus-family hypotheses
\cite[Proposition~6, Eq.~(6), p.~7]{BR}. The source explicitly states the
ordinary-RH specialization for moduli one and two
\cite[Section~3.4, p.~10]{BR}. For \(\mathcal Q(x)=\{1\}\),
\(\varphi(1)=1\), \(\eta(x)=\log x/\sqrt{x}\), and the error is exactly
\[
 \frac{\log\log x}{\log x}+2\frac{\log x}{\sqrt{x}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The formal contract retains an eventual error constant \(C\ge0\), a global
window bound and an attained value identifying \(M_W\). It follows that
\[
 \alpha<\frac{cD_4(F)-\norm F_1}{2M_W}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Good(\alpha,c).
 \tag{6}\label{eq:margin}
\]
This is an actual prime-count consumer. It does not manufacture an explicit
onset from an asymptotic source estimate.

For finite explicit bounds, the generalized zero-summation step is proved
for actual nontrivial zeta zeros with multiplicity. Its source counting input is
\[
 N(T)=\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}\operatorname{mult}(\gamma),\quad
 Q(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi e}+\frac78,\quad
 |N(T)-Q(T)|\le0.15\log T+3\quad(T\ge e),
 \tag{7}
\]
together with the separately printed \(N(10)=0\)
\cite[Section~6.1, Lemma~9 and proof, p.~23]{CMS}.
The arbitrary-function transfer is not attributed to the particular
test-function calculation printed after that lemma. The formal proof first
uses finite-cutoff integration by parts and Fubini, then passes to limits.
For \(g_\varepsilon=\sqrt{F^2+\varepsilon^2}\), it proves
\[
 |g_\varepsilon'|\le |F'|,\qquad
 0\le g_\varepsilon-|F|\le\varepsilon.
\]
The smoothing parameter tends to zero at fixed cutoff before the cutoff
tends to infinity. Actual conjugate-zero multiplicities supply the negative
ordinates; no differentiability of \(|F|\) at its zeros is assumed.

A signed logarithmic moment strengthens the explicit estimate. If
\(F(u)\ge f_0-2\pi^2\mu_2u^2\) on \(0\le u\le\tau\le1\) and
\(|F(u)|\le V_1/(4\pi^2u^2)\) for \(u>1\), the proved even-function bound is
\[
 \int_{\RR}|F(u)|\log|u|\,du
 \le \frac{V_1}{2\pi^2}
 -2f_0\tau(1-\log\tau)
 +4\pi^2\mu_2\tau^3\left(\frac19-\frac{\log\tau}{3}\right).
 \tag{8}\label{eq:signedlog}
\]
The actual applications use \(\tau=3/8\). On \([\tau,1]\) the integrand is
nonpositive; the tail starts at \(1\). Equation~\eqref{eq:signedlog}
uses sign information in the density correction, not cancellation between
different complex zeros.

The explicit prime argument also proves its wing, prime-power, endpoint,
finite-slab and infinite-ray inequalities. The finite slab uses
Montgomery--Vaughan block choice \(16\), the ray choice \(1\), and the
original \(0.0012\) reserve is retained. For example, the entrance allowance
comes from the actual source inequality
\[
 \pi(M+N)-\pi(M)\le\frac{2N}{\log N}
 \quad(M>0,\ N>1),
 \tag{9}
\]
followed, for \(y=b-a+\ell\ge\ell>1\), by
\[
 \#\{a<p\le b\}\le\frac{2y}{\log y}
 \le\frac{2(b-a)}{\log\ell}+\frac{2\ell}{\log\ell}.
\]
The extra entrance term is thus derived rather than inserted as an unexplained
source constant. Concavity controls the full finite log interval and

\begin{theorem}[Flat family and fixed specialization]\label{thm:flat}
For every integer \(m\ge1\), \(\alpha\ge0\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), put
\[
 c_m(\alpha)=\frac{2m}{2m-1}
 \left(\alpha+\frac{1+\log(4m-2)}{\pi}\right).
 \tag{14}
\]
Then \(\Good(\alpha,c_m(\alpha)+\varepsilon)\);
\(\mathcal G_\alpha\ne\varnothing\); and \(C(\alpha)\le c_m(\alpha)\).
Separately, the fixed \(m=64\) rational-slack specialization proves
\[
 \Good\!\left(\alpha,\frac{21}{10}+\frac{128}{127}\alpha\right).
 \tag{15}\label{eq:15}
\]
The coefficient in \eqref{eq:15} is not exactly \(c_{64}(\alpha)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose \(5m+1\) rational nodes: the first \(m\) are one and the remaining
\(4m+1\) are zero. Their actual affine spectrum has
\[
 F(0)=D_4(F)=\frac{2m-1}{m},\quad J(F)=0,\quad M_W=W(0)=1.
\]
The corresponding polynomial is
\(P_m(t)=1+2\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\cos(2\pi jt)\). Set \(r=2m-1\).
The proof establishes, on their stated domains,
\[
 \sin(\pi t)P_m(t)=\sin(r\pi t),\quad
 P_m(1-t)=P_m(t),\quad |P_m(t)|\le r,\quad
 |P_m(t)|\le\frac1{\sin(\pi t)}\ (0<t\le1/2).
\]
Split at \(a=1/(r\pi)\). The small interval contributes at most \(1/\pi\).
On the other interval use
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\log\tan(\pi t/2)}{\pi}
 =\frac1{\sin(\pi t)},\qquad
 \tan(1/(2r))\ge1/(2r)>0.
\]
Continuity and all nonzero denominators are proved before applying FTC.
Reflection gives
\(\int_0^1|P_m|\le(2/\pi)(1+\log(2r))\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:BRpair} and the exact denominator show
\(c_m(\alpha)D_4-\operatorname{cap}=2\alpha\).
Adding any positive \(\varepsilon\) makes \eqref{eq:margin} strict.
Nonemptiness and boundedness below are proved before taking the infimum.

For the fixed specialization the kernel proves
\[
 254<\sum_{k=0}^{14}\frac{(277/50)^k}{k!}\le e^{277/50},
 \qquad \log254<277/50.
\]
Together with \(\pi>3.14\), this gives
\((2/\pi)(1+\log254)<(21/10)(127/64)\).
The additional strict slack proves the coefficient in \eqref{eq:15} itself.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full real-density consequence]\label{thm:realalpha}
Let \(K=\Kflat\). For every \(\eta>0\), for all sufficiently large real
\(\alpha\),
\[
 \Good\!\left(\alpha,\alpha+\frac{\log\alpha}{\pi}+K+\eta\right),
 \qquad
 \varnothing\ne\mathcal G_\alpha,\quad
 C(\alpha)\le\alpha+\frac{\log\alpha}{\pi}+K+\eta.
 \tag{16}
\]
In \eqref{eq:good}, the subsequent prime-variable threshold and the positive
margin may depend on \(\alpha,\eta\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take \(m(\alpha)=\lfloor(\pi/2)\alpha\rfloor_++1\) and
\(r(\alpha)=2m(\alpha)-1\). The formal proof uses the natural-floor ratio limit
to obtain \(r(\alpha)/\alpha\to\pi\) and \(r(\alpha)\to\infty\).
For \(\alpha>0\), exact algebra and the positive-domain logarithm identity give
\[
 \begin{split}
 c_{m(\alpha)}(\alpha)-\alpha-\frac{\log\alpha}{\pi}
 ={}&\frac{\alpha}{r(\alpha)}
 +\frac{1+\log(2r(\alpha)/\alpha)}{\pi}\\
 &+\frac{1+\log(2r(\alpha))}{\pi r(\alpha)}.
 \end{split}\tag{17}
\]
The terms tend to \(1/\pi\), \((1+\log(2\pi))/\pi\), and zero,
respectively; the last uses \(\log r/r\to0\). Thus the left side tends
to \(K\). Its eventual strict upper bound leaves a positive allowance in
Theorem~\ref{thm:flat}, proving the actual GoodCoefficient statement and the
nonempty-infimum conclusion. This is a full real-\(\alpha\) limit, not merely
an integer subsequence. No joint uniform prime-variable onset is deduced.
\end{proof}
